# AI Decision Leadership Curriculum

A four-phase curriculum for AI and data-driven decision leadership, covering decision framing, data and evidence, AI systems and workflows, responsible execution, governance, and business value.

The program is designed around a simple principle:

> AI creates value only when it improves a real decision—and people remain accountable for the evidence, trade-offs, controls, and consequences.

## Who this is for

- Executives and senior leaders
- Strategy, transformation, product, and operations teams
- Data, analytics, and AI professionals
- Risk, legal, security, governance, and assurance teams
- Facilitators building applied AI leadership programs

## The four phases

### Phase 1 — AI decisions and decision framing

Learners identify the real decision, distinguish symptoms from causes, expose assumptions and stakeholders, evaluate whether AI is appropriate, and define human authority.

Primary artifacts include the Root Cause Analysis, Decision Statement, Stakeholder Map, Assumption Log, and AI Decision Suitability Canvas.

### Phase 2 — Data thinking, evidence, and execution

Learners translate decisions into analytical questions, inspect data structure, define metrics, specify how evidence changes action, design experiments, and identify controlled AI opportunities.

Primary artifacts include the Business Question Map, Data Structure Diagram, KPI Catalogue, Decision Specification Document, Experiment Plan, and AI Opportunity Matrix.

### Phase 3 — AI systems, workflows, and business value

Learners move beyond isolated prompts to design bounded assistants, controlled workflows, evaluations, strategic decision processes, reproducible analytics, and evidence-backed business cases.

Primary artifacts include the Prompt Library, AI Assistant Specification, workflow design, evaluation set, Analytics Notebook, evidence register, and AI business case.

### Phase 4 — Sustainable AI decision leadership

Learners build repeatable decision habits, exercise executive judgment under pressure, and integrate governance, risk, security, privacy, assurance, and economics across the AI lifecycle.

Primary artifacts include the Decision Operating System, executive decision record, governance framework, risk register, and final capstone.

## Learning method

The curriculum uses realistic cases, cumulative artifacts, and an **Analyze–Challenge–Verify–Decide** cycle:

1. **Analyze:** form an independent position before using AI.
2. **Challenge:** use AI to identify alternatives, missing assumptions, and weaknesses.
3. **Verify:** check useful suggestions against data, definitions, authoritative sources, and operational constraints.
4. **Decide:** accept, modify, or reject each suggestion and document why.

AI may extend judgment, but it is not treated as the decision owner or source of truth.

## Recommended starting path

1. Open the [Phase 1 Course Guide](phase-1-course-guide.md).
2. Work through the phase's grouped modules in order.
3. Complete each artifact in the accompanying workbook.
4. Use the phase source guide to verify material claims and understand limitations.
5. Continue only after meeting the phase's mastery gates.
6. Complete the Phase 4 final capstone and defend the decision, evidence, controls, and economics.

## Source provenance

Every phase has a dedicated source guide. The [Source Origin Map](source-origin-map.md) provides a repository-wide index of external sources, originating organizations, source classes, and points of use.

Sources include government frameworks, legal texts, international standards, intergovernmental guidance, academic research, professional methods, security resources, and vendor-authored technical guidance. Always verify current versions and applicability before operational use.

## Important limitations

- The curriculum is educational material, not legal, regulatory, security, financial, or professional advice.
- Fictional cases and illustrative figures are not evidence of real system performance or business results.
- Laws, standards, technical guidance, and AI capabilities change; verify current requirements before implementation.
- Organizations remain responsible for jurisdiction-, sector-, workforce-, contract-, and risk-specific analysis.
