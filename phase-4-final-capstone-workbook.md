# Final Capstone Workbook: AI Decision Leadership Portfolio

## Purpose

This workbook integrates the full course into one board-ready portfolio. Use one consequential organizational decision throughout. The artifacts must agree with one another: the KPI must measure the Decision Statement; the experiment must test the business case; the assistant and workflow must implement the governance rules; the operating cadence must monitor the risk register; and the executive memo must accurately summarize the evidence.

The required portfolio contains:

1. Decision Suitability Assessment
2. Decision Statement
3. KPI Framework
4. Experiment Design
5. AI Assistant
6. AI Workflow
7. Prompt Library
8. AI Analytics
9. Business Case
10. Governance Framework
11. Decision Operating System
12. Executive Memo
13. Risk Register
14. Executive Board Defense

# 1. Portfolio rules

## Evidence labels

Use these labels in every artifact:

- **FACT:** directly supported by a cited source or reproducible data.
- **CALCULATION:** derived from shown inputs and formula.
- **INTERPRETATION:** plausible meaning of evidence.
- **ASSUMPTION:** needed for the proposal but not established.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** testable proposed explanation.
- **UNKNOWN:** material gap not resolved.
- **DECISION:** choice made by an authorized owner.

## Version register

| Artifact | Version | Date | Owner | Change | Evidence/reason | Tests rerun | Approver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Claim register

| Claim ID | Claim | Label | Source/calculation | Confidence | Contradicting evidence | Artifact use | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Internal consistency test

Before submission, verify:

- the decision, population, scope, and time horizon match across artifacts;
- metric names have one definition;
- benefit figures reconcile to the financial model;
- risk owners have authority and appear in the operating cadence;
- data described in the assistant/workflow appears in the governance map;
- evaluation thresholds match scale gates and stop rules;
- model/vendor versions match the system inventory;
- residual risks disclosed to the board match the risk register;
- the memo contains no stronger claim than the underlying evidence.

## Analytical sequence

1. Profile data quality and lineage.
2. Reproduce baselines.
3. Separate observation, calculation, interpretation, and recommendation.
4. Verify denominators and time windows.
5. Examine distributions and prespecified segments.
6. Test rival explanations and confounders.
7. Conduct robustness and sensitivity analysis.
8. Independently reproduce central results.
9. Document limitations and evidence gaps.

# 13. Artifact 12 — Executive Memo

Maximum two pages plus appendices.

## Required structure

**To:** Executive Board  
**From:** [learner]  
**Decision requested:** [one sentence]  

### Recommendation

State Act, Delay, Redesign, or Do Not Proceed, including scope and timing.

### Why now

State problem, strategic relevance, and cost of delay.

### Evidence

Give only the pivotal verified evidence, denominators, segments, and uncertainty.

### Economics

Show range, assumptions, total cost, benefit realization, and downside.

### Risks and people affected

Disclose material inherent and residual risks, distributional effects, and unresolved issues.

### Operating and governance model

Name Decision, Data, Execution, System, Risk, and stop authorities.

### Conditions and next gate

State funding, scope, controls, thresholds, owners, deadline, and review.

### Alternatives rejected

Name the strongest alternative and why it is not recommended.

### Ask

Specify the exact authorization required from the board.

## Memo red-team

- Which sentence is stronger than the evidence?
- What bad news is buried?
- Which number lacks a denominator?
- What must the board believe for the economics to work?
- What would a harmed customer or employee say is missing?
- Could a board member misunderstand “human oversight” or “accuracy”?

# 16. Pre-board readiness gate

The learner must answer “yes” with evidence:

- Is there one precise board decision?
- Has the strongest non-AI alternative been evaluated?
- Are material metrics independently reproducible?
- Are denominators, time windows, and segments consistent?
- Does the experiment support the claimed causal mechanism?
- Does the assistant pass predefined critical tests?
- Has the workflow been failure-tested end to end?
- Are data purpose, access, retention, and vendors approved?
- Is human oversight operationally feasible?
- Are fairness, transparency, explanation, and remedy tested?
- Are incidents, fallbacks, and shutdown authority exercised?
- Are benefits realizable and owned?
- Are residual risks within authorized appetite?
- Can the organization exit or retire the system?
- Does the memo faithfully summarize all of the above?

If any answer is “no,” either close the gap, narrow the ask, or recommend Delay/Redesign. Do not conceal it.

# 17. Executive board simulation

## Format

- 10 minutes: learner presentation;
- 56 minutes: eight roles, seven minutes each;
- 10 minutes: closed board deliberation;
- 10 minutes: decision and conditions;
- 4 minutes: learner response and commitment.

The learner may bring the two-page memo and appendices. Any number not traceable within two minutes is treated as unsupported.

## CEO — strategic coherence

Aggressive questions:

- Why is this one of our top three uses of management attention?
- What decision gets better, not merely faster?
- Why AI? What would process redesign achieve?
- What are you asking me to believe that you have not proven?
- If this fails publicly, why was the risk worth taking?
- What will you stop funding to do this?
- What is the smallest version that preserves strategic value?
- What would make you recommend terminating your own program?

Conflict inject: The CEO wants a market announcement before the pilot is complete.

## CFO — economic integrity

Aggressive questions:

- Which savings reach the financial statements?
- Are “hours saved” released capacity, avoided hiring, or fiction?
- Show the denominator behind unit savings.
- What is the downside case if adoption is half your forecast?
- Where are review, assurance, remediation, and exit costs?
- Have benefits been double counted across teams?
- What happens when vendor price or volume doubles?
- Why should we fund this rather than the non-AI alternative?

Conflict inject: Finance will approve only if year-one payback is claimed; evidence supports 24–30 months.

## CTO — architecture and reliability

Aggressive questions:

- Which part requires nondeterministic AI?
- How do you reproduce a decision after model and source changes?
- What is the failure mode when a tool call partly succeeds?
- How do permissions propagate through retrieval and actions?
- What tests run before a model or prompt change?
- What is our fallback during provider outage?
- How long and how much would vendor exit take?
- Where does human judgment enter, and is that a bottleneck?

Conflict inject: CTO prefers the strategic platform vendor; task tests show a smaller vendor performs better.

## Legal — rights and liability

Aggressive questions:

- What is our role and obligation in every jurisdiction?
- On what authority is each data category used?
- What did affected people reasonably expect?
- Who owns inputs, outputs, and derivative artifacts?
- Can we preserve privilege and litigation evidence?
- What contractual protection exists for infringement, breach, and change?
- Can a person challenge and correct a material outcome?
- Which fact, if wrong, changes your legal conclusion?

Conflict inject: Legal finds the design defensible but warns the proposed marketing language is misleading.

## Compliance — control evidence

Aggressive questions:

- Which obligations map to which controls?
- Show that the control operated, not that a policy exists.
- Who approves exceptions and when do they expire?
- How are changes reclassified and reapproved?
- Can we identify every active use and owner?
- What evidence will be available to an auditor?
- Which control has failed in testing?
- Are we relying on vendor certification for our own compliance?

Conflict inject: Compliance demands full review of every case, destroying the economic case.

## Operations — workflow reality

Aggressive questions:

- What happens on Monday morning at peak volume?
- How many exceptions will reach my team?
- What work did your time study exclude?
- Which local policy or segment breaks the standard workflow?
- What will frontline staff do when the system is wrong but fast?
- Who owns backlog during an outage or safety pause?
- How are training, support, and process changes funded?
- What happens to released capacity?

Conflict inject: Operations can meet launch date only by removing two proposed review controls.

## Security — adversarial resilience

Aggressive questions:

- Draw the trust boundaries and show the highest-privilege path.
- How does an attacker use retrieved content to manipulate the system?
- What sensitive data appears in prompts, embeddings, and logs?
- Can the model take action with the user’s full permissions?
- What evidence would let us scope an incident?
- Which adversarial tests failed?
- How quickly can we revoke, isolate, and rotate?
- What does the vendor owe us during an incident?

Conflict inject: Security discovers a high-severity unresolved finding during the meeting.

## Skeptical investors — credibility and moat

Aggressive questions:

- Is this durable advantage or a vendor feature anyone can buy?
- Why will employees and customers trust it?
- What evidence is independently verifiable?
- How concentrated is the model, cloud, and data dependency?
- What regulatory or reputation event could erase the value?
- What capability remains if the vendor disappears?
- Are you scaling learning or scaling risk?
- Why should we trust management’s claims after the metric corrections?

Conflict inject: Investors support growth only if management accepts more automation than the risk review recommends.

# 18. Board scoring and decision

Each role scores 0–4:

- recommendation clarity;
- evidence integrity;
- response to challenge;
- strategic/economic logic;
- stakeholder and ethical judgment;
- control credibility;
- ownership and execution readiness;
- candor about uncertainty.

Aggregate the role scores by dimension. A board outcome of **Approved** requires:

- no valid hard stop;
- an overall average of at least 3.2;
- no scoring dimension below 3;
- explicit acceptance of material residual risk by an authorized owner.

**Conditionally approved** is available only when the remaining items are limited, do not include a hard stop, and have named owners, deadlines, restricted scope, and a later approval gate. **Redesign** applies when the value may be real but the decision, evidence, workflow, economics, or controls are structurally weak. **Rejected** applies when the use is unsuitable, unlawful, economically indefensible, or outside risk appetite.

## Hard stops

Any role can propose a hard stop, but must identify the violated obligation, risk appetite boundary, or missing critical control. The chair rules whether it is valid.

## Deliberation questions

1. Is the decision suitable and strategically important?
2. Are pivotal claims verified?
3. Is value plausible and realizable?
4. Are affected people and material harms addressed?
5. Are critical controls effective?
6. Are ownership and execution credible?
7. Is the proposal reversible enough for remaining uncertainty?
8. What conditions, if any, are necessary?

# 19. Course completion decision

The mentor declares completion only when:

- the board outcome is **Approved**;
- every hard stop is cleared;
- no scoring dimension is below 3;
- all conditions from an earlier conditional decision are evidenced as complete;
- the learner can explain one material revision caused by challenge;
- the final portfolio is internally consistent and versioned.

If the proposal is rejected, the learner must decide whether to redesign the use case or defend a disciplined “do not proceed” recommendation. Correctly stopping an unsuitable AI initiative can demonstrate stronger leadership than forcing approval.

The board may approve a well-supported **Do Not Proceed** recommendation. In that case, “Approved” means the decision recommendation survived scrutiny; it does not mean an AI deployment was authorized.

## Post-board reflection

**Which challenge most changed the proposal?**  
**Where did confidence exceed evidence?**  
**Which stakeholder carried risk without adequate voice?**  
**Which governance control created economic tension?**  
**What did you stop, narrow, or redesign?**  
**What decision habit will you institutionalize?**
