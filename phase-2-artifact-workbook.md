# Phase 2 Artifact Workbook

## Data Thinking, Evidence, and AI Execution

Use this workbook to build one evidence-to-execution portfolio across Modules 3–7. Work on one realistic organizational decision throughout the phase. Each artifact becomes an input to the next; do not treat the six artifacts as independent assignments.

The required portfolio contains:

1. Business Question Map
2. Data Structure Diagram

The module source chapters remain the authoritative teaching references. This workbook is the participant-facing place to complete, review, and revise the artifacts.

# Part A. Workbook rules and project setup

## A1. Non-negotiable learning method

### Prefer realistic scenarios over theory

Use a real decision or a credible scenario containing actual numbers, definitions, constraints, competing explanations, and consequences. Do not complete an artifact with generic statements that cannot be tested against evidence.

### Analyze before asking AI

For every artifact, use this order:

1. **Analyze:** inspect the scenario, data, and source material independently.
2. **Commit:** record an initial interpretation, assumptions, and provisional choice.
3. **Challenge:** use AI only to test the work for omissions, alternatives, or weaknesses.
4. **Verify:** check AI suggestions against data, definitions, authoritative sources, and operational reality.
5. **Decide:** accept, modify, or reject suggestions and document why.

If the independent-analysis section is blank, the artifact is incomplete.

### AI must not replace critical thinking

AI may act as a critic, counterargument generator, or clarity assistant. AI is not:

- the decision owner,
- an authoritative source,
- evidence that a claim is true,
- a substitute for calculations,
- a substitute for examining records,
- authorized to choose values, risk tolerance, or trade-offs.

The participant remains accountable for every conclusion.

## A2. Evidence labels

Use these labels consistently:

- **FACT:** directly supported by a cited source or reproducible record.
- **CALCULATION:** derived from displayed inputs and formula.
- **INTERPRETATION:** plausible meaning of evidence.
- **ASSUMPTION:** claim required for the work but not established.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** testable proposed explanation or effect.
- **UNKNOWN:** material gap not yet resolved.
- **DECISION:** choice made by an authorized owner.

# Part I. Final review and oral defense

## I1. Portfolio readiness checklist

### Question and evidence

- [ ] Decision is real and owned.
- [ ] Analytical method matches the claim.
- [ ] Correlation is not presented as causation without credible design.
- [ ] Evidence labels are used consistently.

### Data and metrics

- [ ] Entity, grain, keys, relationships, time, and denominators are explicit.
- [ ] Joins reconcile and comparisons are like-for-like.
- [ ] KPIs are reproducible, balanced, and resistant to obvious gaming.
- [ ] DSD action, fallback, trade-off, and reversal rules are complete.

### Validation and execution

- [ ] Validation method fits the evidence need.
- [ ] Treatment, counterfactual, metrics, and decision rules are pre-specified.
- [ ] Ethics, uncertainty, heterogeneity, and full impact are addressed.
- [ ] AI opportunities came from workflow analysis and beat non-AI alternatives.
- [ ] Human authority, appeal, monitoring, recovery, and stage gates are practical.

### Learning method

- [ ] Every artifact contains independent pre-AI analysis.
- [ ] AI challenged rather than originated the reasoning.
- [ ] AI suggestions were verified.
- [ ] Accepted and rejected suggestions are documented.
- [ ] Participant can defend the work without AI assistance.

## I2. Oral defense questions

1. What did you observe before forming an explanation?
2. Why is this the right analytics type?
3. Which competing explanation is most threatening?
4. What does one row represent, and where can joins overcount?
5. Why does each KPI deserve attention, and how could it be gamed?
6. What evidence would cause a different decision?
7. What is the counterfactual, and why is it credible?
8. What result would be inconclusive rather than negative?
9. Why is AI better than the strongest non-AI alternative?
10. Which judgments remain human, and can people realistically exercise them?
11. What triggers pause, rollback, or stop?
12. Which AI suggestion did you reject, and why?
